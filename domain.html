Academic Foundation

1. Literature Survey

A systematic review of current paradigms in assistive mobility reveals diverse techniques for indoor localization, obstacle perception, and feedback systems. These studies establish the theoretical foundation for our multi-modal wearable platform.

Indoor Navigation & Positioning Approaches

Plikynas et al. [4] conducted an extensive taxonomy of indoor navigation technologies mapped against the sensory needs of visually impaired individuals. López-de-Ipiña et al. [8] validated marker-based indoor navigation utilizing RFID tags and QR codes. While effective in structured environments, their utility degrades in unprepared architectures. Chen et al. [11] investigated wearable semantic visual SLAM, demonstrating accurate trajectory estimation but demanding substantial computational and battery overhead.

Wearable Perception & Object Detection

Y. Chen et al. [2] presented a wearable assistive system combining quantized YOLOv3 object detection with stereo depth measurement and haptic tactile feedback. Redmon et al. [3] established the foundational unified real-time YOLO framework. Subsequent research demonstrated that integrating distance sensors (such as LiDAR [1]) significantly improves spatial awareness over monocular 2D vision alone.

Implications for the Smart Spectacle Prototype

The literature proves that relying solely on a single sensing modality (e.g., vision alone or ultrasound alone) fails under diverse indoor lighting and varying obstacle geometries. Our project bridges this by combining optical vision, micro-LiDAR, and 6-axis inertial tracking into a single unified edge system.

Critical Deficiencies in Existing Systems

2. Research Gap

While isolated solutions exist for object detection or RFID-based navigation, a comprehensive analysis highlights several critical research gaps in current assistive wearables.

Infrastructure Independence

Most existing indoor positioning systems require dense physical beacons, Wi-Fi fingerprint databases, or pre-mapped QR codes. When entering unmapped buildings, these systems fail completely. There is a lack of lightweight, offline dead-reckoning architectures that function reliably without prior setup.

Edge Compute vs. Power Constraints

Complex visual SLAM algorithms demand high-performance GPU hardware that is too heavy and power-hungry for continuous spectacle-mounted use. Existing research rarely balances real-time hazard detection with a lightweight, wearable form factor operating on low battery capacity.

Disconnection Between User & Guardian

Assistive wearables commonly focus solely on localized alerts, completely ignoring remote caregiver awareness. When a visually impaired person experiences a severe fall, medical distress, or disorientation, existing devices provide no automated telemetry to emergency contacts.

Focus of Our Investigation

Our project directly fills this gap by developing an integrated IoT-based smart spectacle that performs offline dead-reckoning positioning and edge vision hazard detection locally, while establishing an asynchronous telemetry bridge to a remote guardian whenever connectivity is present.

Problem Statement

3. Research Problem

According to the World Health Organization (WHO) [5], over 2.2 billion individuals globally live with near or distance vision impairment. Indoor mobility remains their most persistent daily challenge.

The Environmental & Physical Dilemma

Navigating unfamiliar indoor complexes (e.g., academic buildings, hospitals, transport stations) is fraught with hazards:

  • Absence of GPS satellite signals due to structural wall attenuation.
  • Overhead hazards, half-open doors, and stairs that traditional canes cannot sense early.
  • Dynamic moving hazards such as passing pedestrians and trolleys.
  • High cognitive load when interpreting ambiguous tactile or audio notifications.

Core Research Questions

To solve this multi-dimensional challenge, our research investigates:

  • RQ1: How can multi-sensor data (LiDAR + IMU + Optical) be fused efficiently on an energy-constrained wearable spectacle?
  • RQ2: Can accurate indoor trajectory tracking be sustained offline through dead-reckoning and landmark cues?
  • RQ3: How can edge AI models detect common indoor hazards with sub-100ms latency on low-power hardware?
  • RQ4: How can remote caregiver monitoring and emergency fall alerts be integrated without sacrificing battery life?
Project Goals

4. Research Objectives

The overarching goal of the project is to develop and evaluate a cost-effective, dependable assistive smart spectacle system that enhances independent indoor mobility and security.

Obj 1

Wearable Hardware & Sensor Bus

Design, 3D-print, and evaluate an ergonomic spectacle frame housing the TF-Luna LiDAR module, optical camera, and 6-axis IMU. Establish an efficient I2C/UART bus architecture delivering low-latency audio cues via a non-intrusive sound synthesizer.

Obj 2

Offline Hybrid Indoor Positioning

Formulate an offline positioning framework combining IMU step and heading tracking (dead-reckoning) with computer vision landmark recognition, minimizing cumulative drift to within ±0.5 meters over typical indoor journeys.

Obj 3

Remote Guardian & Safety Telemetry

Build a secure cloud/mobile guardian ecosystem that continuously receives telemetry, automatically detects impacts/falls, monitors prolonged inactivity, and sends real-time emergency push notifications with location data.

Obj 4

Edge Vision Danger Detection

Train, quantize, and deploy a custom lightweight neural network capable of classifying indoor obstacles, downward staircases, and drop hazards with >85% mAP, fused with LiDAR distance measurements for prioritized acoustic alerts.

Research Approach

5. Proposed Methodology

The research follows the Design Science Research Methodology (DSRM), progressing through four iterative phases from theoretical formulation to controlled experimental evaluation.

Phase 01

Requirement Analysis & Data Sourcing

Conduct literature analysis, extract user accessibility guidelines, and curate an annotated image dataset of typical indoor hazards (staircases, low walls, doors, obstacles).

Phase 02

Component Architecture & Modeling

Independently engineer the 4 modules: 3D hardware chassis, dead-reckoning algorithm, guardian cloud API, and quantized object detection pipeline.

Phase 03

System Integration & Sensor Fusion

Synchronize sensor streams on the edge microcontroller. Implement EKF sensor fusion, distance-priority thresholding, and acoustic alert arbitration.

Phase 04

Empirical Testing & Validation

Execute structured benchmark trials in controlled indoor environments at SLIIT to record latency, accuracy, positioning drift, battery endurance, and usability.

Local Edge Independence vs. Cloud Synchronization

In our architecture, life-safety functions (obstacle avoidance, dead reckoning, acoustic guidance) run 100% locally on the wearable frame with zero network latency. Cloud communication is used exclusively for non-blocking telemetry updates to the guardian application.

Engineering Stack

6. Technologies Used

The project utilizes a cohesive selection of embedded hardware components, computer vision frameworks, and telemetry protocols optimized for power efficiency and responsiveness.

Embedded Hardware

  • Microcontroller: ESP32-S3 Dual-Core Xtensa LX7 (Wi-Fi + BLE 5.0)
  • Distance Sensor: Benewake TF-Luna Micro-LiDAR (0.2m - 8m range)
  • Inertial Sensor: MPU-6050 6-Axis Gyroscope & Accelerometer
  • Optical Sensor: OV2640 2MP Camera Module
  • Audio: Bone-conduction transducer / Piezo acoustic buzzer
  • Battery: 3.7V 1200mAh Lithium Polymer with TP4056 BMS

Machine Learning & Vision

  • Detection Model: Quantized YOLOv8-Nano (INT8 precision)
  • Vision Library: OpenCV & Edge Impulse SDK
  • Training Framework: PyTorch & Google Colab GPU
  • Feature Extraction: ORB / FAST corner detection for landmark matching
  • Filtering: Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) for sensor fusion

Guardian Cloud & Mobile

  • Backend Service: Node.js runtime with Express & WebSocket
  • Messaging Protocol: MQTT / HTTPS REST API
  • Guardian Dashboard: Responsive HTML5, CSS3, Vanilla JS
  • Telemetry Database: Firebase Realtime Database / MongoDB
  • Alerts: Push Notifications & automated SMS fallback
